Nominated Proof-of-Stake: How Polkadot Selects and Rewards Validators

Published August 5, 2026 · Updated August 14, 2026 · DOT Oracle Guide Editorial Team

What happened

Polkadot secures its relay chain with Nominated Proof-of-Stake (NPoS). DOT holders may nominate up to a set number of validator candidates; an election algorithm then selects the active validator set and distributes stake behind it as evenly as possible. Validators produce blocks and participate in finality, while nominators share both rewards and penalties.

Why it matters

NPoS is the mechanism that also underpins everything built on top of Polkadot, including oracle deployments on parachains: the cost of attacking a data feed secured by the relay chain is tied to the total stake behind the validator set. Understanding staking therefore helps readers reason about the security of the applications they use.

Risks and caveats

Staking is not a savings product. Staked DOT can be slashed if a chosen validator misbehaves, unbonding takes a fixed period during which the tokens remain exposed to price movements, and reward rates vary with network conditions. Nothing in this article should be read as encouragement to stake; it describes a protocol mechanism.

Market context

Most major proof-of-stake networks — Ethereum, Cosmos, Cardano — use different validator-selection and delegation designs. Polkadot's election-based approach aims to equalize stake across validators rather than letting it concentrate on the most popular ones.
