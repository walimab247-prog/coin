Agile Coretime on Polkadot: A New Way to Buy Blockspace

Published July 2, 2026 · Updated August 10, 2026 · DOT Oracle Guide Editorial Team

What happened

Polkadot has completed its transition from two-year parachain slot auctions to “agile coretime”, a model in which projects buy relay-chain computation (cores) either in bulk for a month at a time or on demand, block by block. The change was part of the broader “Polkadot 2.0” direction approved through on-chain governance and rolled out across Kusama and Polkadot.

Why it matters

Under the auction model, teams had to lock large amounts of DOT for years before shipping anything. Coretime lowers that barrier: a small team can rent capacity for a month, prototype, and scale up only if needed. For oracle providers specifically, on-demand cores make it cheaper to run low-frequency data-publishing chains without paying for idle blockspace.

Risks and caveats

Coretime pricing is market-driven and can fluctuate, which makes long-term cost planning harder than under fixed leases. Secondary markets for reselling coretime are still young, and tooling continues to change. None of this implies anything about the price of DOT; it is an infrastructure-economics change.

Market context

Other ecosystems are exploring similar “blockspace as a commodity” ideas, including Ethereum's blob-fee market introduced with EIP-4844. Polkadot's implementation is distinctive in selling generalized execution cores rather than data capacity alone.
